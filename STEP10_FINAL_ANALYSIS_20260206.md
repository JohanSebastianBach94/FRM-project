## 2) Comparison with the stress-testing literature

### Where this aligns well
- **Canonical shock space**: operating in standardized factor innovations is standard practice in market/portfolio stress testing and is compatible with a time-varying risk model (your DCC/ADCC layer). This is consistent with the “shock space → propagate” paradigm widely used in both academic and supervisory toolchains.
- **Regularized inverse problem**: “pick factor shocks to match a small set of narrative constraints” is essentially a constrained/penalized regression / minimum-norm solution. Using ridge-style regularization is a defensible way to avoid unstable, extreme solutions when the mapping is ill-conditioned.
- **Governance + audit**: persisting the λ-grid, selection rule, and diagnostics is stronger than many ad-hoc scenario implementations.
- **Explicit uncertainty disclosure**: surfacing negative out-of-sample R² and treating it as low-confidence is aligned with good model risk governance.

### Where this differs from typical FSAP/IMF macro stress frameworks
- In many FSAP/IMF implementations, macro outcomes are generated by a **macro model** (VAR/DSGE/semi-structural) and then pushed into **satellite models** (PD/LGD, balance-sheet, funding spreads, etc.).
- Your pipeline instead uses a reduced-form mapping (Step 4) and then solves an inverse problem to hit macro deltas.

That’s not “wrong” — it’s a different layer in the stack:
- What you have is closer to a **market-factor stress engine with macro narrative anchoring**.
- If later you add country macro models/satellites, Step 10.2 can become either (a) a calibration tool, or (b) a consistency check layer.

### Feasibility: adding a true FSAP/IMF-style “macro model → satellites” stack
This is feasible, but the practical choice of macro model matters a lot.

- **Most practical starting point (recommended)**: a small **semi-structural / BVAR** layer that generates quarterly paths for the core FSAP variables per country:
  - core macro: `gdp_growth_yoy`, `unemployment_rate`, `cpi_infl_yoy`, `policy_rate`
  - optionally add global drivers: oil, global risk (VIX/V2X), global rates, global GDP
  - output: scenario-consistent macro paths + uncertainty bands (useful for governance)

- **VAR vs BVAR vs DSGE (what’s realistic here)**:
  - **VAR**: easiest to implement; can be fragile with short samples/many variables.
  - **BVAR (Minnesota / shrinkage priors)**: best cost/benefit; stable in high dimension; common in supervisory-style toolchains.
  - **DSGE**: highest effort and model risk burden (identification, priors, steady-state); usually not worth it unless you need a structural narrative decomposition.

- **Satellite models (the FSAP/IMF “credit and balance-sheet” layer)**:
  - PD/NPL: logistic / hazard / panel regressions using macro paths + financial conditions
  - LGD: macro + collateral proxies (house prices, rates) + sectoral effects
  - Net interest income / funding spreads: policy rate + yield curve + risk indices
  - Trading/market losses: your existing factor shock engine already covers this well

- **How this fits your current pipeline (minimal disruption)**:
  1) Macro model produces macro scenario paths (replaces the hand-authored narrative deltas).
  2) Satellites consume those macro paths to produce balance-sheet outcomes.
  3) Step 10.2 can remain as the **translator** that maps macro paths into a governed market-factor shock space (for portfolios / DCC/ADCC risk), or as a **consistency check** when the macro model implies a story that the market-factor space cannot reproduce without implausible z.

- **Implementation effort (order-of-magnitude)**:
  - BVAR macro layer: ~1–3 weeks to get a governed MVP (data prep, estimation, stability tests, outputs).
  - 1–2 satellites (PD + NII/spreads): ~2–6 weeks depending on portfolio granularity and available panel history.
  - Full FSAP-style balance sheet: longer (often months) because data + accounting identity integration dominates.

### The main economic red flag: extremely large factor z for “slow-moving” series
When translating the stored daily shocks back into quarterly z (the same quantity plotted in Panel 2), the maximum |z| by ISO/scenario is very large for some countries:
- DEU severe: max |z| ~ 52.6
- FRA severe: max |z| ~ 36.4

The culprit factors are debt-to-GDP ratio series (e.g., `GC.DOD.TOTL.GD.ZS_DEU`, `GC.DOD.TOTL.GD.ZS_FRA`). These are not “return-like innovations” and do not move meaningfully at daily frequency.

Implication:
- Mechanically the inversion can use them to fit unemployment/CPI, but economically they behave like structural state variables.
- Showing these as quarterly z-shocks in a market-style shock panel is likely misleading.

Related symptom:
- The diagnostics z-cap is 6.0, but a material share of quarter/factor points exceed it. This indicates the current auto-λ selection is not enforcing a hard plausibility bound; it is selecting λ to meet fit tolerances, even if that implies extreme z on certain factors.

## 4) Recommended updates (highest value)

### A) Enforce “canonical shock space” for Step 10.2 factor universe
Goal: ensure factors used in inversion are genuinely shockable innovations.
- Exclude slow-moving structural ratios (e.g., `GC.DOD.TOTL.GD.ZS_*`) from the inversion factor set.
- Exclude lagged feature columns (e.g., `*_lag1..lag5`) from inversion; keep lags for Step 4 prediction if needed, but don’t treat them as independent shock coordinates.

Expected benefit:
- Much more plausible z magnitudes, cleaner economic story, better alignment with your Step 9 governance statement (“return-like innovations”).

### B) Make plausibility constraints binding (not just reported)
Goal: stop solutions that require implausible z.
Options:
- Hard rule in auto-λ selection: reject λ if `max|z| > z_cap` (or if too concentrated), then pick best remaining.
- Or switch to a bound-constrained solve (projected / clipped / quadratic programming variant).

### C) Increase macro constraint coverage in a governed way
Right now, some ISOs only constrain 1 variable (e.g., DEU only unemployment; ITA only CPI). That invites degeneracy.
- Add mappings for policy rate / equity / sovereign spread where available and where Step 4 test R² is acceptable.
- Consider a small “global anchor” constraint set (VIX/V2X, equity, funding, spread) that is common across ISOs.
